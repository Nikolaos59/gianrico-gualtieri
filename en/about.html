Biography

Background

Gianrico Gualtieri was born in Naples on 10 February 1962. He currently lives and works in Saint-Étienne, France.

After completing an artistic baccalaureate in 1979, he attended architecture school and the Academy of Fine Arts. Since the 1980s, he has researched the techniques of old painting, first studying the Italian school of the seventeenth century and later the Flemish and Dutch schools.

He has made numerous copies and now devotes himself to his own work, with a particular fondness for flower still lifes and vanitas.

Training

Technique as a path of learning

Independent research into old pictorial techniques with Ciro Paolillo in Portici (Naples), covering seventeenth-century painting techniques and the restoration of paintings; workshop training with Angelo Romano in painting restoration.

In-depth independent study of the supports, materials and methods used by seventeenth-century painters, especially Flemish and Dutch artists. Development of a painting medium based on old formulas.

Preparing colours from powdered pigments. Using glazes, or “velature”, for the highest quality; mastering several techniques: pure glazes, monochrome underpainting with glazes, half-impasto, impasto and more. This includes methods specific to flower painting, such as the spot technique.

Work

Paintings and skills

He has painted many works of his own, chiefly flowers and vanitas, now held in private collections around the world. One of his vanitas paintings belongs to the collection of Vittorio Sgarbi, former Italian Minister of Culture.

He has made copies of Old Master paintings and still accepts commissions. He also restores oil paintings, relining them when needed; works on the restoration of mural decoration; and can create trompe-l’œil, faux wood and faux marble finishes.

Inspiration

The poetry of the image

The key concept in the artist’s inspiration is undoubtedly technique, conceived not as a mere means but as an integral part of the poetry of the image.

Against a widespread view that prioritises the “message” and relegates technique to a secondary, merely expressive means, the artist believes that recovering, cultivating and educating one’s technique deepens the very essence of the image and of painting. Painting should increasingly coincide with the process of its making, without intellectual mediation.

Exhibitions / activity

A practice shaped over time

  • Group exhibition at Galerie d’Hermance, Switzerland, 2003.
  • “Portrait de Fleurs”, at Rosa Gallica, Reims, 1–29 December 2012.
  • Resident artist at Galerie du Cardo, Reims, until 2015.

He paints for collectors around the world. One of his vanitas paintings is in the collection of Vittorio Sgarbi, former Italian Minister of Culture. His works are always signed.
